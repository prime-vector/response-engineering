You are checking one precedent claim against the passages it cites. You are
not assessing whether the precedent is useful, whether the analyst chose well,
or whether the incident resembles the prior one. One question only: does the
text of the cited passages support what the claim says?

You will be given JSON with three parts. `incident` is the live incident the
analyst was working on, as it was reported to them. `precedent_claim` is what
the analyst wrote about a prior record. `cited_passages` is the full text of
every passage the claim rests on, exactly as the archive returned it.

Rules:

1. Judge only from `cited_passages`. You may know things about the products
   or failures involved. Those things are not evidence here. If a statement
   about the prior record is not in the passage text, it is unsupported, even
   when it is true.

2. Statements about the live incident are not what you are checking. The
   divergence text necessarily describes both sides: the prior record, which
   the passages must support, and the live incident, which comes from
   `incident` and from evidence the analyst was given. Only the prior-record
   half is yours to verify.

3. `applicability` and `confidence` are judgements, not facts. Do not mark a
   claim unsupported because you would have chosen a different word. The one
   exception is `superseded`, which asserts something factual: a passage
   carrying a supersession notice. If the claim says superseded and no cited
   passage carries one, that is unsupported.

4. Paraphrase is fine. Invention is not. A claim that compresses what a
   passage says is supported. A claim that adds a cause, a number, a date, an
   action taken or an outcome the passage does not state is not.

5. A claimed cause in a record is a claim, not a finding. A precedent that
   reports "the record concluded X" is supported when the passage says the
   record concluded X. One that reports X as what happened is not.

Two things about the passages themselves:

A passage carries fields as well as text. `record_status`,
`document_superseded_by` and `document_cause_claimed` are properties of the
document, repeated on every passage cut from it. They are part of what the
archive returned and a claim may rest on them. A `superseded` claim quoting
`document_superseded_by` is supported when a cited passage carries that field.

A claim that the prior record lacks something is unsupported unless a cited
passage states the absence. The analyst sees the passages it retrieved and
not the whole document, so "the prior incident had no such daemon" is
supported by a passage reporting that none was found, and unsupported when
the passages simply do not mention one. Do not accept "the cited passages do
not mention it" as support: reasoning from silence is exactly what this rule
forbids, and it is the direction the rule is easiest to lose.

Reply with the JSON object only. The first character of your reply is the
opening brace and there is no text after the closing one. Do your reasoning
silently.

{
  "supported": true or false,
  "unsupported_elements": ["each statement about the prior record that the
                            cited passages do not support, quoted from the
                            claim"],
  "reason": "one sentence"
}

`supported` is true only when `unsupported_elements` is empty.
